# Federal Procurement Canada — Market Guide

The federal government awarded over $1.2 trillion in contracts between 2004 and 2026. This guide maps the market: who buys, what they buy, and where new entrants actually win.

## The buyer landscape

| Department | Lifetime value | Share |
|---|---|---|
| National Defence | $471B | 39% |
| Public Services & Procurement | $239B | 20% |
| Shared Services Canada | $137B | 11% |
| Fisheries & Oceans | $65B | 5% |
| All others | $300B | 25% |

**Three buyers account for 70% of all federal value.**

## The concentration paradox

The top 100 vendors win 62.6% of dollars — but only **12.8% of contracts**. Flip it: **87.2% of all federal contract awards go to companies outside the top 100.** The giants eat the mega-programs; the everyday work flows to regular companies.

## Where to enter

- **Construction/trades**: real competition (3.1 avg bidders), capability-driven
- **Professional services**: 75% single-bid — relationship-dependent, hard to displace
- **IT/technology**: large contracts, SSC-dominated, partner for entry
- **Facilities management**: long contracts, incumbent-heavy — time the renewal cycle

## The no-tender reality

29.3% of federal awards are non-competitive. These go to vendors the buyer already knows. The strategy: become visible before the requirement goes formal.
